    What the engine believes, and why

    Every rule in the calculator maps to a finding below. Where the evidence runs out, the app says so instead of guessing. Sources are the papers and reviews we read; nothing here is padded.

    Bottle concentration caps: 3% / 6% / 8%

    A small amount of carbohydrate (1–3%) speeds fluid absorption, while drinks at 8% and above delay fluid delivery. Glucose + fructose blends soften that at higher concentrations. GSSI SSE #244, Baker (2023)

    Fluid delivery was compromised once concentration went above 6%, and adding sodium didn't fix it. Evans et al., 2009

    Repeated drinking of an 8% carb beverage during exercise significantly slowed gastric emptying; lower concentrations didn't. IJSNEM, 1999

    → Hot: 3% (fluid first). Moderate: 6%. Cold: 8%, and the app will not go higher. Our old 10% cold band was a tolerance habit, not evidence.

    Heat lowers how much carb you can use

    Cyclists at 35°C vs 16°C oxidized less of the carbohydrate they drank and burned 25% more muscle glycogen. Jentjens et al., J Appl Physiol 2002

    Runners in the heat used ~20% less ingested carbohydrate even with hydration kept normal. Mougin et al., 2025

    Water emptied from the stomach more slowly in a hot environment, likely from reduced gut blood flow. Fluid Replacement and Heat Stress (NASEM)

    → The "Heat: lower carb target ~15%" toggle. Off by default; the app nudges you in the Hot band.

    How many carbs per hour, and of what kind

    A single carbohydrate type tops out around 60 g/hr because its intestinal transporter saturates; glucose + fructose together can push oxidation to ~105 g/hr. Jeukendrup, Eur J Sport Sci 2008

    ~60 g/hr for 2–3 hr efforts; ~90 g/hr for ultra-endurance, only with multiple transportable carbohydrates. Jeukendrup, Sports Med 2014 · GSSI SSE #106

    → Red warning if a single-source product is used above 60 g/hr. Note above 90 g/hr. The library tags each product's carb type.

    Fluid and sodium

    ACSM: drink to keep body-water loss under 2%, typically 400–800 mL/hr depending on sweat rate and size, with sodium around 0.5–0.7 g per liter. Sweat rates vary so much that a measured sweat test beats any formula. ACSM Position Stand, 2007

    For events over ~4 hr, replace 50–80% of the sodium you lose in sweat. McCubbin, via CTS

    Warmer air raises sweat rate ~0.04 L/hr per °C, but real prediction needs wind, sun and humidity that weather apps don't reliably give. Exp Physiol 2023 · J Appl Physiol 2024

    → Fluid checks at 0.4 / 0.8 / 1.2 L/hr. A sweat-rate field in Profile, not a weather formula. "Feels-like" temperature is the one weather input because your weather app already folds in humidity and wind.

    Heat stress: WBGT, not just feels-like

    Wet bulb globe temperature is the heat-stress index used by ACSM, OSHA, the U.S. military and NWS. It combines temperature, humidity, wind and solar radiation; heat index and feels-like ignore the sun. NWS Tulsa: WBGT · NWS: WBGT vs heat index

    Our estimate uses the same method as NWS's national WBGT forecast: the Dimiceli–Piltz black-globe equation with the Hunter–Minyard natural wet bulb, fed by Open-Meteo's hourly temperature, humidity, wind and solar radiation. NDFD WBGT algorithm (Boyer, NWS/MDL)

    For U.S. locations the app first asks NWS for its own operational WBGT forecast (an NDFD element since June 2022) and uses that; elsewhere, or if NWS doesn't answer, it estimates with the same method. The finding says which one you got. NWS Service Change Notice 22-43

    → When you use the weather lookup, the concentration band is set by average WBGT over your ride window (defaults: Hot ≥ 80°F, Cold < 60°F WBGT). Typed temperatures still use the feels-like thresholds. It's a forecast-based estimate, not a globe thermometer; shade, pavement and local wind move the real number.

    Medications and heat

    Clinician guidance groups heat-sensitizing drugs by mechanism: SSRIs/SNRIs (impaired central thermoregulation, more sweating, dehydration and electrolyte risk), tricyclics and antipsychotics (reduced sweating), ADHD stimulants (more heat production), beta blockers (impaired skin blood flow), ACE inhibitors and GLP-1 agonists (blunted thirst), diuretics and lithium (fluid and electrolyte loss), first-generation antihistamines (reduced sweating). Americares / Harvard C-CHANGE provider guide · NYC Health: Heat and Medications · Drug Topics summary

    The drug-name lookup uses the consolidated list from the Arizona Heat Preparedness Network's Heat and Medications sheet for providers (ADHS, Pima County, UArizona), which draws on CDC's clinician guidance. CDC: Heat and Medications — Guidance for Clinicians

    A 2024 meta-analysis of 35 trials found the commonly cited classes don't carry equal risk, and there is no evidence for a specific fueling adjustment per drug. summary

    → The app flags the mechanism and what to watch; it never changes a number because of a medication. Talk to your prescriber about training in heat.

    Sodium follows sweat

    Sweat sodium concentration is an individual trait (roughly 200–2,000 mg/L; ~1,000 is typical) and is what a sweat test reports. Hourly loss is that concentration times sweat volume, so it rises with heat, and concentration itself creeps up at higher sweat rates as ducts reabsorb less. Baker, 2017

    → Profile stores mg/L; the plan multiplies by today's fluid, so a Hot-day fluid bump raises the sodium target with it. Drinking more plain water at a fixed sodium intake is the recipe for dilutional hyponatremia.

    Caffeine

    The ISSN position stand: 3–6 mg/kg taken about 60 minutes before it is needed improves endurance performance; doses above ~9 mg/kg add side effects, not benefit. Guest et al., 2021

    Plasma caffeine peaks 30–60 minutes after a dose and has a half-life of roughly 5 hours, so a dose in the first hour still works in hour four but is fading. Late-exercise doses restore performance when fatigue sets in. Cox et al., 2002

    → Short rides get the caffeinated gel first. Rides over 2.5 h get the first dose so its window covers the back half (about 90 minutes in on a 4-hour ride), then one more every ~2.5 h if the rider's per-ride cap allows. Nothing after the rider's cutoff time. The app never adds caffeine unless it is switched on in Profile, and never adjusts carbs or sodium for it.

    Timing

    Serial small drinks empty steadily; big boluses of strong solution do not. NASEM review

    → Minimum gels per hour and even spacing. The exact "1 per hour" and "first gel at 20 min" are practice, not a paper.

    What we don't have evidence for

    The exact temperature breakpoints (85°F, 65°F) are ours. Whether 8% is fine for you on a cold day is your gut. Per-person sodium needs vary several-fold. Use the journal.

    Coach's guidance

    From Bluebird Multisport: aim for 75 g/hr on easy rides and 85 g/hr on long rides with intervals. Don't plan below 75 — the difference in recovery and how you feel is real.

    Disclaimer

    Bluebird Fuel is a planning tool, not medical advice.
